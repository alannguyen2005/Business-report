# Data Storytelling Principles

## 1. Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto)
```
                ┌─────────────┐
                │  BIG ANSWER │  ← Answer first
                └──────┬──────┘
          ┌────────────┼────────────┐
     ┌────▼────┐  ┌────▼────┐  ┌───▼────┐
     │ Arg 1   │  │ Arg 2   │  │ Arg 3  │  ← Key arguments
     └────┬────┘  └────┬────┘  └────┬───┘
     ┌────▼────┐  ┌────▼────┐  ┌───▼────┐
     │Evidence │  │Evidence │  │Evidence│  ← Data/facts
     └─────────┘  └─────────┘  └────────┘
```

- Top of pyramid = conclusion (SCQA Answer)
- Each section = supporting argument
- Individual findings = evidence
- Reader can stop at any level and understand

## 2. SCQA in Slides
SCQA maps to deck structure — NOT to a single summary slide:
| SCQA | Deck Position |
|------|--------------|
| S | Cover subtitle + Business Context slide |
| C | "What's Happening" headline (tension point) |
| Q | Analysis Framework slide framing |
| A | Key Takeouts slide (What / Why / So What) |

## 3. Headline = Insight Rule
Every slide headline must be an insight statement:
- Action verb required
- ≤15 words (must fit on maximum 2 lines)
- Never use colons `:` or em-dashes `—` to separate parts of the headline
- Reader understands the point without looking at the chart

| BAD (label / with colon) | GOOD (insight / clean) |
|-------------------------|------------------------|
| "Revenue by Segment Q1 2026" | "Mid-Market churn drives 60% of revenue decline" |
| "Monthly Ticket Volume: Support Spiked" | "Support tickets spiked 55% in June as volume grew" |
| "Customer Retention Analysis" | "New customers churn 3x faster than returning segment" |

## 3.1 Card Bullet Points & Supporting Narrative Rules
When writing card bullets or panel narratives:
- **Bold Noun Phrase Prefix:** Always start the paragraph/bullet with a bolded noun phrase prefix that begins with an article (e.g. `**The primary target**`, `**The leverage point**`, `**The recalibration trigger**`).
- **No Separators:** Do not put `:` or `—` immediately after the bold markdown tag. The text must flow naturally.
  - *Correct:* `**The target segment** Enterprise accounts showed...`
  - *Incorrect:* `**The target segment**: Enterprise accounts showed...` or `**The target segment** — Enterprise accounts showed...`

## 4. Progressive Revelation
Story unfolds in order:
1. **Hook** — tension that creates curiosity (cover)
2. **Context** — what's normal (business context)
3. **Tension** — what changed (complication)
4. **Investigation** — deep dives (root causes)
5. **Resolution** — answer + recommendation (takeouts)
6. **Impact** — what happens next (forecast/projection)

Never reveal the conclusion before building the case.

## 5. Breathing Room
Every 3-4 insight-heavy slides, insert a transition:
- Section opener (dark background, section title)
- Key stat callout
- Summary card before next deep dive

This prevents cognitive overload.

## 6. "So What" Callout Rule
Every chart slide must include a "So what:" callout:
- Starts with "So what:"
- Contains ≥1 specific number
- ≤20 words, 1 sentence
- Explains WHY this chart matters, not WHAT it shows
