The 2025 apparent -8.3% annual revenue decline is entirely explained by a Q1 2025 external demand shock (Jan-Mar), not a structural business deterioration.

**Why:** Q1 2025 revenue collapsed -35.7% vs Q1 2024 simultaneously across all 25 store-category combinations; volume fell -35%, price/unit was unchanged; Apr-Dec 2025 was flat +0.18% vs 2024; Q1 2026 fully recovered to $36.8M. Q1 shortfall = $13.06M = 99.7% of the total $13.1M annual gap.

**How to apply:** When presenting 2025 performance, always isolate Q1 anomaly. Normalized 2025 (excluding anomaly) was -0.03% vs 2024. No further investigation of 2025 decline is warranted unless stakeholders ask specifically about the Q1 2025 root cause (external event).

Key structural drivers:
- Nov-Dec holiday season: +52% vs rest-of-year average (accounts for 20% of total revenue)
- Weekend premium: +30% vs weekday average (weekends = 34.3% of revenue, 28.7% of days)
- Electronics: 27% category share, $26,434 avg/row vs $12,921 for Home & Garden

Definitively ruled out: category mix shift (shares stable ±0.06pp), promotion erosion (lift grew 2023-2025), store divergence as variance driver (Store_West gap is stable structural condition, not a change driver).

2026 annualizes at +2.8% vs 2025 on comparable Jan-Jun basis (+26.6% same-period vs 2025).
