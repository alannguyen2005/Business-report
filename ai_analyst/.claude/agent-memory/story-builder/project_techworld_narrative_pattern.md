For TechWorld `techworld_data_sample` analysis runs, the core narrative paradox is:

**The framing:** Revenue *looks* stable (flat ~$60K/month, p=0.32 on trend) but one product (Smartphone Alpha) is quietly eroding the base at ~$34K/year.

**Why this framing works:** It creates tension between the aggregate metric (flat revenue = no alarm) and the sub-product story (one SKU accelerating toward zero). Executives who see aggregate dashboards would miss this entirely. The diagnostic layer reveals that ~80% of the -4.1% YoY decline traces to a single SKU.

**Thread across all sections:**
- Descriptive → flat revenue, Electronics concentration at 87.7%
- Diagnostic → Smartphone Alpha volume collapse confirmed in all 4 regions simultaneously (rules out regional/operational failure), pricing stable (rules out price war)
- Predictive → Q2 2026 forecast ~$152K assumes Smartphone Alpha trend continues; wide CIs (±$12.5K) driven by 2025 data gap

**Key numbers to anchor:**
- Q2 2026 total: ~$152K ($48.8K Apr / $49.6K May / $53.6K Jun)
- Best model MAPE: 3.24% (Linear trend + seasonal adjustment), beats baseline by 71.2%
- Smartphone Alpha: -30.6% FY22→23, -64% H1 2024 annualized
- Electronics share: 87.7% — single-category concentration risk
- Net Profit Margin: locked at 33% throughout — demand problem only, not a cost problem

**How to apply:** When re-running this analysis or updating with new data (especially 2025 data if obtained), maintain the same narrative paradox framing. If 2025 data becomes available, re-emphasize that the CIs tighten substantially and the model confidence improves from Grade B (78) to potential Grade A.
